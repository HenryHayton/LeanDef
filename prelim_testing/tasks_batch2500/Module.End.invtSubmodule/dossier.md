## VTask.invtSubmodule

### Object

Given a module endomorphism `f : M → M` (a linear map from a module to itself), `VTask.invtSubmodule f` is the collection of all *`f`-invariant submodules* of `M`, organised as a sublattice of the lattice of all submodules of `M`. A submodule `p` is called `f`-invariant if `f` maps every element of `p` back into `p` — that is, `f(p) ⊆ p`. The result is a sublattice because the supremum (sum) and infimum (intersection) of two `f`-invariant submodules are again `f`-invariant.

`VTask.invtSubmodule : {R : Type u_1} -> {M : Type u_2} -> [Semiring R] -> [AddCommMonoid M] -> [Module R M] -> (f : Module.End R M) -> Sublattice (Submodule R M)`

`R` is the scalar semiring over which the module is defined. `M` is the underlying additive commutative monoid carrying the `R`-module structure. The three instance arguments supply the semiring structure on `R`, the additive commutative monoid structure on `M`, and the `R`-module structure linking them. The explicit argument `f` is the module endomorphism whose invariant submodules are being collected.

### Conventions

No special junk-value or boundary conventions are declared: the definition is a total construction that is meaningful for every module endomorphism over any semiring and module satisfying the stated type-class hypotheses.

### Worked examples

- Claim: The zero submodule belongs to `VTask.invtSubmodule f` for any endomorphism `f`, because `f` maps `0` to `0`.

- Claim: The top submodule (the whole module `M`) belongs to `VTask.invtSubmodule f` for any endomorphism `f`, because `f` maps elements of `M` into `M`.

- Claim: For a module endomorphism `f`, if `p` and `q` are both in `VTask.invtSubmodule f`, then their join (the submodule `p ⊔ q`) is also in `VTask.invtSubmodule f`.

- Claim: For a module endomorphism `f`, if `p` and `q` are both in `VTask.invtSubmodule f`, then their meet (the submodule `p ⊓ q`) is also in `VTask.invtSubmodule f`.

- Claim: A submodule `p` is a member of `VTask.invtSubmodule f` if and only if `f` maps `p` into itself (i.e., `Set.MapsTo f p p`).

### Boundaries

- The zero submodule is always `f`-invariant (since `f 0 = 0`), so it is always a member of the sublattice.
- The top submodule (all of `M`) is always `f`-invariant, so it is always a member of the sublattice.
- The sublattice is closed under arbitrary suprema and infima that exist within it, and in particular under pairwise joins and meets; the sublattice structure guarantees exactly this closure.
- When `f` is the zero endomorphism, every submodule is invariant, so `VTask.invtSubmodule f` equals the full lattice of submodules.
- When `f` is the identity, every submodule is invariant, again giving the full submodule lattice.

### Not to be confused with

- `Submodule.comap f p` — the preimage of a single submodule `p` under `f`; membership in `VTask.invtSubmodule f` asks that `p ≤ Submodule.comap f p`, but `comap` itself is just one submodule, not a collection.
- A *`f`-stable* or *`f`-cyclic* submodule generated by a single orbit — those are specific elements that may or may not belong to `VTask.invtSubmodule f`, not the entire sublattice.
- `Representation.invtSubmodule ρ` (for a group representation `ρ`) — that collects submodules invariant under every `ρ g` simultaneously, whereas `VTask.invtSubmodule f` concerns invariance under a single fixed endomorphism `f`.
