# Updated Reward Structure

*Design of record for candidate scoring · originally 21 July 2026, consolidated 24 July 2026 — see Changelog.*
*Supersedes the fact/mutant scoring design in all earlier documents where they conflict.*

## Changelog

- **2026-07-20** — Reward structure defined within the architecture report: decidable facts as the backbone; proof-obligation facts (topologies, compactness-like objects) adjudicated by a flagship LLM prover via Bedrock, confined to evaluation and task authoring — excluded from the training loop, whose reward had to come from the decidable layer only (`verifier_architecture_2026-07-20.md` §2, §4).
- **2026-07-21** — Reward structure promoted to its own design-of-record document. Fact suite reframed as three types — decidable casework, membership facts, global theorem facts — each with its own adjudication path and priority ordering; authoring-time mechanical validation made load-bearing (a fact that can't be certified against the ground truth doesn't exist); definition-mutation battery adopted as a provisional authoring-time sanity check, not a scored component.
- **2026-07-24** — The tiered adjudication ladder (decide → pinned tactics → hammer → equivalence transfer → Bedrock flagship) becomes the certification mechanism for every proof-mechanism fact, in training as well as evaluation and authoring; the "expensive work stays on the far side of the line from training" stance from 21 July is removed. Added: verified-only admission split (candidate-level vs. task-level, with an interaction rule for high-UNKNOWN batches), a proof-script cache, the statement-format rule by mechanism, flagship-tier caps/parity/aim policy, axiom discipline for ladder-produced proofs, unified ladder-budget configuration, authoring-time tier-of-discharge recording, adaptive sampling allocation by trailing pass rate, a reliability requirement carried from batch-4 evidence, and the pre-registered pilot measurement plan.

## 0 · Scope and the one-line summary

This document fixes what a candidate definition is scored on and how the facts that produce that score are chosen and adjudicated. The admissibility gate is unchanged from the existing design and restated here only for completeness.

**The reward is a single quantity: the number of facts the candidate passes.** Nothing else enters it. Everything below is either machinery for making that number trustworthy, or policy for choosing which facts exist. As of 24 July 2026 (see Changelog), the adjudication machinery is the same five-tier ladder (§3) in every scoring context — training, evaluation, and authoring-time validation — for every fact type; no fragment of the fact suite is carved out of the training reward.

---

## 1 · Layer 0 — Admissibility (unchanged)

Before scoring, a candidate must clear the standing eligibility gate: it elaborates against the pinned signature; contains no `sorry` (detected as a REPL warning, per the proven Step 0 harness); introduces no new axioms; does not shadow the pinned name or tamper with dependencies. Where a fact requires the candidate side to supply a proof, the same gate applies to that proof. Admissibility is pass/fail eligibility and contributes nothing to the score.

---

## 2 · The fact suite — three types

All reward flows through facts. A fact is a concrete proposition about the candidate, stated in terms of the pinned name, and it is either **certified** (kernel-checked, by computation or by proof) or **not**. Fidelity — the fraction of the suite certified — is the reward. Three types of fact make up a suite.

### 2.1 · Decidable casework

Concrete, pointwise, kernel-computed checks: `tau 6 = 4`, `tau 16 = 5`, membership of a specific element, a specific classification of a specific finite structure. Milliseconds each against the warm environment, deterministic, no completeness gap, kernel-pure.

**Granularity principle: cases are kept individual, never bundled.** A bounded window such as `∀ n < 100, tau n = ref n` is logically stronger than its instances, but as a *reward component* it is one bit — a candidate wrong at a single edge case scores identically to one wrong everywhere. Unbundled cases give the same coverage with two properties the bundle destroys: the failure pattern is immediately legible (exactly *which* edge cases broke — the diagnostic view that drives fact-suite repair and mutant analysis), and the reward is graded rather than cliff-shaped, so nearly-right candidates are distinguishable from badly-wrong ones. Bundled windows may still be used at authoring time as internal checks, but they are unrolled into individual cases before anything reaches the reward.

### 2.2 · Membership facts (instance classification)

Facts asserting that the candidate correctly classifies concrete **candidate objects**: correct instances it must accept, and deliberately near-miss instances — objects broken in one known, controlled way — it must reject. The near-miss instances are generated by perturbing true instances (break exactly one axiom of a valid structure) and each is tagged with the property it violates, which makes the resulting negative fact *diagnostic*: it tests precisely one clause of the definition. Negative facts also carry their own witness by construction — the perturbation that made them — so their adjudication is anchored before any candidate is seen.

**Decidable membership is favoured wherever available.** For finite instances and computable predicates these facts are ordinary `decide` checks and live in the fast tier. Where the instance or the signature forces a genuine proof (infinite carriers, `Prop`-valued predicates), the proof is attempted via the adjudication ladder (§3, tiers 2–5). Membership proofs at concrete instances are shallow and mechanical — exhibit the violating witness, or discharge a finite conjunction — so for this class, and this class only, a failed proof search is treated as strong evidence of falsity: the expected UNKNOWN rate is near zero, and the standard tri-state adjudication (fact proved / negation proved / neither) is retained mainly as bookkeeping. This confidence is a property of the *shallowness of the class*, not of the adjudication mechanism, and does not extend to §2.3.

### 2.3 · Global facts (genuine theorems)

Facts capturing behaviour that no amount of casework reaches: identities and properties over the object in general — a proof of a known identity about `tau n` for all n, a structural law, a closure property on an infinite carrier. These are, when certified, **the most reliable checks in the system** — a single global fact constrains the candidate across its whole domain — and simultaneously **the most expensive**, since each is a real proof search: seconds to minutes, nondeterministic, with a genuine UNKNOWN failure mode.

They are therefore governed by a depth discipline. The selecting agent proposes a global fact only when it judges the proof to be short; it must not propose deep theorems, for two compounding reasons: proof-search compute scales with depth, and — worse — reliability degrades, because a prover failing to find an existing long proof converts a true fact about a faithful candidate into a lost reward. Every proposed global fact is validated at authoring time by proving it of the *true* definition under the same budget the candidate side will get; facts the ladder cannot discharge for the ground truth are rejected or simplified before the task ships. Global facts are certified strictly by kernel-checked proof; the ladder (§3) only ever finds proofs, it never adjudicates by opinion.

**Weighting stance.** Global facts are wanted wherever they are cheap, but they are not favoured over the first two types. Their share of a suite floats with supply: where decidable casework and membership facts are plentiful, a suite may carry very few global facts or none; where the object's nature thins the first two classes (abstract concepts, infinite-carrier structures), global facts are prioritised more heavily because they are what remains.

### At a glance

| | Adjudication | Cost per fact | Failure modes | Default priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Decidable casework** | `decide`, kernel | milliseconds | none beyond falsity | first |
| **Membership** | `decide` where possible; ladder tiers 2–5 otherwise | ms (decide) / tier-dependent (proof) | near-zero UNKNOWN | first (decidable) / second (proved) |
| **Global facts** | kernel-checked proof via the adjudication ladder (§3, tiers 2–5) | seconds – minutes, tier-dependent | UNKNOWN; depth-sensitive | third; rises when others are thin |

## 3 · The adjudication ladder

Every proof-mechanism fact — membership facts that force a genuine proof, and every global fact — is adjudicated by the same five-tier ladder, regardless of scoring context (training, evaluation, or authoring-time validation). Kernel-only certification is unchanged: the ladder changes who *searches* for proofs, never who *adjudicates* them.

1. **Tier 1 — `decide`**: unchanged; reuses `harness/` as built.
2. **Tier 2 — pinned deterministic tactic set**: `rfl`, `omega`, `simp`, `exact?`, `aesop`, plus the `norm_num`/`positivity` class, under fixed per-tactic budgets. The batch-4 discharge instrument (`miner/discharge.py`) is the ancestor of this rung; its generic core (`attempt_statement`) is the extraction point.
3. **Tier 3 — hammer**: LeanHammer, or Lean-auto + Duper with the fact's anchor theorems as explicit premises. Both configurations are measured in the pilot (§11); the better one is adopted. Runs only on the EC2 environment (x86; pinned toolchain v4.32.0, which matches LeanHammer's newest supported release tag).
4. **Tier 4 — equivalence transfer**: attempt candidate = truth (or ↔) via tiers 2–3; success transfers the entire fact suite.
5. **Tier 5 — Bedrock flagship**: last resort, hard-capped per round (§6).

### 3.1 · Statement format by mechanism

- A **`decide`-mechanism** fact's canonical `statement` is the full runnable command, exactly as today.
- A **`proof`-mechanism** fact's canonical `statement` is a bare Prop. The ladder wraps it as a goal in a standard template (`theorem <fact_id> : <Prop> := by <attempt>`); the cache (§3.2) stores scripts against the bare-Prop statement hash. Schema v1.1 (§12) encodes this rule and its validators enforce it.

### 3.2 · Proof-script cache (requirement, not optimization)

Search happens once; every re-encounter is replay. Replay is plain kernel checking and runs anywhere, including the development Mac; only novel-goal search requires the EC2 environment.

**Cache record format.** Per certified fact-proof: statement hash (of the canonical statement form, §3.1), toolchain pin (Lean + Mathlib rev), discharging tier, the reconstructed proof script, the proof's axiom closure (§3.3), and wall-clock of the original search. The cache key is (statement hash, toolchain pin); a re-pin invalidates the cache by construction.

**Replay failure.** A cached script that fails replay (should be rare; indicates environment drift or a defective reconstruction) demotes the fact to UNKNOWN for that scoring pass and flags the cache entry for re-search. Replay failures are logged and counted; a nonzero rate is a review item.

### 3.3 · Axiom discipline for ladder-produced proofs

Every proof produced by any tier — including tier-2 tactics, tier-3 reconstructions, and tier-5 LLM proofs — passes a no-`sorry` and axiom-closure check before its fact is marked certified. The permitted-axiom set for **fact proofs** is the standard Mathlib triple (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`) and is **distinct from the candidate's own admissibility baseline** (§1), which continues to govern the candidate declaration alone. A proof exceeding the permitted set fails certification (the fact stays UNKNOWN) and is logged. The axiom closure is recorded in the cache entry (§3.2) so replay never re-litigates it. Not yet implemented in this codebase (confirmed by the read-only repo audit) — tracked under Consequential work, §12.

## 4 · Verified-only admission

Two rules, stated separately.

### 4.1 · Candidate-level (epistemics)

No candidate enters training data with any unresolved proof-fact UNKNOWN. A candidate whose fact suite contains UNKNOWNs after the full ladder (within budget) is excluded from that round's training data. Exclusion is not a negative label: the candidate is neither rewarded nor penalized; it is absent.

### 4.2 · Task-level (economics)

Every task included in a training round has a complete funded reward channel for that round: every fact in its suite must be adjudicable within the round's configured ladder budgets (§7). Inclusion-without-reward is forbidden. A task whose adjudication cannot be funded in a given round rotates out for that round; rotation is not deletion.

### 4.3 · The interaction rule (pre-registered action for pilot measurement #4, §11)

A funded task can still produce high-UNKNOWN candidate batches. The response depends on the correlation structure, which the pilot measures:
- UNKNOWNs spread across candidates and facts (prover incompleteness): drop the affected candidates (§4.1) and keep the task.
- UNKNOWNs concentrated on specific facts across many candidates (suite defect): pull the task for fact-suite repair; the defective facts are re-authored or removed before the task returns. A task exceeding the `EXCESSIVE_UNKNOWN_THRESHOLD` (`harness/config.py`) on the same fact(s) in two consecutive rounds is pulled automatically.

## 5 · Choosing the facts

Fact selection is performed by an **LLM agent at data-collection time**, per task. Its brief:

1. **Read the object and classify its regime.** Computable with cheap evaluation → casework-rich. Finitely instantiable concept → membership-rich. Abstract or infinite-carrier → global-fact-leaning, decidable pre-filters where any exist.
2. **Assemble the portfolio under the priority order** of §2: decidable casework and decidable membership first; proved membership next; global facts to cover what the others structurally cannot, subject to the depth discipline, with their weight raised only when the first two classes are thin.
3. **Place casework deliberately.** Individual cases are cheap, so spend them where errors live: boundaries, degenerate inputs (n = 0, empty structures, identity elements), and the witness points of known near-misses — not uniformly over easy interior points.
4. **Propose, never certify.** Every selection is validated mechanically before the task ships: decidable facts are executed against the true definition; proof-requiring facts are proved of the true definition under the production budget (§3, §7). A fact the machinery cannot certify for the ground truth does not exist. The agent's judgment steers effort; it never bears soundness.

### 5.5 · Authoring-time integration

- **Tier-of-discharge recorded at authoring.** When a fact is validated at authoring time, the tier that certified it is recorded (schema v1.1 field, §12).
- **Automation-first fact selection — capped, soft.** Fact selection may prefer facts that cheap tiers discharge at authoring time, as a soft preference with a cap; richness and coverage (§2) still dominate. This dial is held until pilot evidence (§11).
- **Anchors are machine-consumed.** Global facts' anchor theorem lists feed tier-3 explicit premises (§3). Anchor quality (resolvable, specific) is validated at authoring. Prerequisite: mining output does not currently persist mentioning-theorem names; the miner extension to persist them (§12, item 2) precedes or accompanies stage-2.

## 6 · Flagship tier: caps, parity, aim

- **Hard cap.** Tier-5 attempts per round are capped by configuration (count and dollar ceiling). Exhausting the cap leaves remaining facts UNKNOWN; it never blocks the round.
- **Budget parity.** Per-round tier-5 spend is bounded by the round's authoring spend. Scoring never quietly becomes the dominant cost.
- **Aimed allocation.** The capped budget is spent preferentially on facts of **non-verbatim plausible candidates that tier 3 failed** — the population where recovered reward most directly counters verbatim bias. Verbatim-identical candidates get tier-5 attention only from budget surplus.
- **Model pin (as of 24 July 2026, corrected against the actual repo state — see note below).** `AUTHORING_MODEL_ID` (`bedrock/config.py`) is pinned to `eu.anthropic.claude-sonnet-4-6`, region `eu-west-1`, following the 24 July diagnostic round trip that confirmed the account has entitlement to that model but not (yet) to `eu.anthropic.claude-sonnet-5` (HTTP 403 AccessDeniedException, "not available for this account" — an entitlement gap, not an IAM permissions gap: no caller ARN appears in that error, unlike a genuine permissions denial). `FLAGSHIP_MODEL_ID` remains pinned to `eu.anthropic.claude-sonnet-5` pending a decision on whether to move it to the same confirmed-working model — **the two slots are not currently pinned to the same model**, which "does tier-5 need a stronger model" leaves open as intended, but by accident of the diagnostic rather than by design. If the pilot leaves a fat residue of hammer-failed facts that the authoring-pinned model also cannot close, the pre-registered follow-up is a one-off re-run of exactly that residue with a flagship-class model for comparison.

## 7 · Ladder budgets as unified configuration

One configuration object governs all ladder execution: per-tactic budgets for tier 2, hammer wall-clock and premise-count for tier 3, equivalence-attempt budget for tier 4, tier-5 caps (count, dollars), and per-fact total wall-clock. It replaces both `harness.config.PROOF_TIMEOUT` (`harness/config.py:39` — unused placeholder, retired) and generalizes the miner's `DISCHARGE_*` dials (which remain scoped to the measurement instrument until the instrument is retired into the tier-2 rung). Budgets are configuration, not schema: task.json reserves a ladder-budget override slot (schema v1.1, §12) but the defaults live in one config file the EC2 worker and the Mac replay path both read.

---

## 8 · Sampling allocation policy

Per-task candidate sampling is **adaptive by trailing pass rate** with floors and caps: tasks with mid-range trailing pass rates (maximal signal) receive more samples; saturated and hopeless tasks decay toward the floor; every included task receives at least the floor every round (consistent with §4.2). Cold start is uniform. The previously mentioned 32 samples/task was a **placeholder, not a decision**; the pilot (§11) fixes the initial values, and changes thereafter follow the one-dial-on-evidence rule.

---

## 9 · Definition mutation — status: provisional

Definition-level mutation (perturbing the *spec* of the definition and formalizing the misreading, per the established pipeline) is **not yet adopted as a standing component**, and if adopted it enters nowhere near the reward. Its candidate role is deliberately modest: a quick authoring-time check that the assembled fact suite is not trivially passable — run the suite against a small battery of wrong definitions and a stock of degenerate candidates (constant functions, vacuous predicates) and confirm the suite fails them. In particular it is the designated catch for **vacuous definitions that would pass the facts trivially**, the failure mode the n=1 probe demonstrated empirically. A suite that a vacuous or known-wrong definition passes is repaired or discarded before the task ships; no number from this check ever touches a candidate's score.

Whether the full spec-mutation pipeline earns a permanent place — versus this minimal degenerate-battery check — is left open, to be decided on the evidence of the first mined batches.

## 10 · Reliability requirements for the ladder worker

Batch-4 evidence (`docs/harvest_review_batch4.md`) is the cautionary precedent: the discharge instrument's *first* run had no environment-death recovery at all, and one dead shared environment after only 9 of 727 definitions cascaded into 718 straight failures before the bug was caught and fixed. The fix now in place (`miner.discharge._attempt_statement_with_recovery`) reimports once and retries the whole statement — but it is still only a single retry per call: if that one reimport doesn't itself succeed, the call returns the pre-recovery failure and hands the caller the same (possibly still-dead) environment id, leaving recovery for the rest of the run dependent on some *later* call independently triggering and succeeding at its own reimport. The ladder worker must not inherit this single-retry design unexamined. Requirements:
- Heavy tiers (3, 5) isolated from the shared base environment, or recovery made a budgeted loop (more than one reimport attempt before giving up on a statement).
- Environment-death recorded as its own status, distinct from FAILED/UNKNOWN, so tier discharge rates (§11) stay clean — a death is an infrastructure event, not evidence about the fact.
- The two-stage split (does the goal elaborate? / what does the ladder do with it once it does) recorded per fact.

## 11 · The pilot (~100 definitions) — pre-registered measurements

The first training run doubles as the pilot. Measurements, each with an owner decision it informs:

1. Tier discharge cascade, truth-side (what fraction of authoring-time facts each tier certifies) → tier-2 tactic-set tuning; hammer adoption depth.
2. Tier discharge cascade, candidate-side → ladder budget defaults (§7).
3. Candidate-side discharge on manufactured non-verbatim-correct candidates → verbatim-bias risk; tier-5 aim policy (§6).
4. UNKNOWN rate and correlation structure → §4.3 actions.
5. False-negative rate on known-subtle-wrong candidates → fact-suite quality bar.
6. Wall-clock and dollars per candidate → sampling allocation (§8), cap levels (§6), EC2 sizing.

Plus the hammer-vs-explicit-premise configuration comparison (tier 3) and, conditionally, the flagship-model residue comparison (§6).

## 13 · What this fixes, in one paragraph

A candidate is admitted by Layer 0, then scored by one number: facts passed. The suite behind that number is built from three fact types in a deliberate priority — granular decidable casework and instance-membership facts as the cheap, kernel-pure backbone; shallow membership proofs where finiteness runs out; sparing, depth-disciplined global theorems where only general behaviour will do — assembled per task by an LLM agent whose every choice is mechanically validated against the ground truth before the task exists. Suite sharpness is checked, provisionally, by an authoring-time battery of wrong and degenerate definitions that must all fail. Every proof-mechanism fact, in training as much as in evaluation and authoring, is adjudicated by the same tiered ladder (§3) under per-round budgets (§7) — the expensive tiers are capped, not confined to a separate offline phase. The reward stays graded, legible, and kernel-certified end to end; cost is controlled by budget caps and tier ordering (cheapest mechanism first), not by walling proof search out of training entirely.
